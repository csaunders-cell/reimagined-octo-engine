Field guide

Real analysts look things up. Use this the way you would use a policy binder: to check a fact, not to replace your judgment.

Who is responsible for what

  • Developer (the EU AI Act says “provider”): builds or substantially modifies the system. Owns design choices, training data, testing, and documentation.
  • Operator (“deployer”): runs a system under its own authority. Owns use as intended, human oversight, monitoring, and telling affected people.
  • User: the person at the keyboard. Follows policy, checks outputs, keeps restricted data out of prompts.
  • Roles shift. Rebrand or substantially change a vendor’s system and you can take on the developer’s duties.

EU AI Act

  • Prohibited practices include social scoring, exploiting people’s vulnerabilities, and emotion recognition at work or school. Banned since 2 February 2025.
  • High-risk uses (Annex III) include hiring, credit scoring, and life and health insurance pricing. Duties cover risk management, data governance, documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy, and security. They apply from 2 December 2027; AI inside regulated products follows on 2 August 2028.
  • Transparency duties (Article 50): tell people they are talking to an AI (applied from 2 August 2026), and mark AI-generated content as synthetic (applies from 2 December 2026 under the 2026 Digital Omnibus).
  • Minimal risk: no specific duties. Spam filters live here.
  • General-purpose model duties have applied since 2 August 2025. Fines for prohibited practices reach €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover.

United States

  • No comprehensive federal AI statute. The 2023 executive order on AI was revoked in January 2025.
  • Executive Order 14365 (December 2025) directs federal agencies to challenge state AI laws. An executive order cannot repeal a state statute.
  • Existing law applies to AI as to any tool: ECOA and Regulation B (credit), FCRA, Title VII (employment), FTC Act section 5, GLBA (financial data), HIPAA (health data).
  • State and local examples: New York City Local Law 144 (bias audits for hiring tools), Illinois Human Rights Act amendments (in force 2026), Colorado SB 26-189 (replaces the 2024 Colorado AI Act; in force 1 January 2027).

Frameworks

  • NIST AI RMF (voluntary): Govern sets culture, policy, and accountability. Map establishes context and identifies risks. Measure tests and tracks them. Manage prioritizes and treats them.
  • ISO/IEC 42001: a certifiable AI management system standard. ISO/IEC 23894 gives AI risk management guidance.
  • OECD AI Principles and the UNESCO Recommendation: international ethics baselines.
  • DoD AI ethical principles: responsible, equitable, traceable, reliable, governable.

Fairness testing

  • Selection rate = selected ÷ applicants, per group. Impact ratio = a group’s rate ÷ the highest group’s rate.
  • The four-fifths rule flags ratios below 0.80. It is a screening rule of thumb, not a legal safe harbor.
  • Dropping the protected column does not remove bias; proxies such as ZIP code carry it.

AI threats and the CIA triad

  • Confidentiality: membership inference, model extraction, sensitive information disclosure.
  • Integrity: data poisoning, backdoors, evasion inputs, prompt injection that changes behavior.
  • Availability: resource-exhaustion queries, vendor outages.
  • Catalogs: MITRE ATLAS; OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications.

Incident response

Prepare; detect and analyze; contain, eradicate, and recover; review afterward. For AI, containment usually means rolling back to a known-good model or a non-AI fallback.
