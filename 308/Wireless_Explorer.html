Handoff Explorer

A phone travels along a road between two cell towers. A handoff is when the network passes the phone from one tower to the next so the connection stays up. Change the settings, then drive the road and see whether the handoff goes smoothly.

  • Move the position slider, or press Drive the whole road. The drive always goes left to right.
  • Turn on signal fading, then try a handoff margin of 0, then 6. Compare what changes.
  • Turn fading off and raise the margin to 20. See what happens when the phone waits too long to switch.

    How this model works

    Signal strength falls as the phone moves away from a tower. The model uses a simple distance rule: every tenfold increase in distance costs 32 dB. The phone stays on its current tower until the other tower is stronger by the handoff margin. If the signal on the current tower falls below the minimum usable level, the call drops. Fading adds smooth random-looking bumps, up to about 6 dB, to each tower's signal. A switch back to the previous tower within 100 m counts as ping-pong.

    Signal Tradeoff Lab

    A radio link can send data faster or more reliably, but not both for free. Choose a modulation (how bits are packed into the signal), move away from the tower, and see how speed and error rate change.

    • Start with QPSK at 100 m, then switch to 256-QAM. What happens to speed and errors?
    • Move to 250 m. Which modulation still meets your target error rate?
    • Raise the spreading factor and watch what you gain and what you give up.

      How this model works

      Received power drops with distance using the chosen environment's rule. Noise power depends on channel width, so a wider channel carries more data but lets in more noise. The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is received power minus noise power. Spreading adds a gain of 10 times the log of the spreading factor to the SNR, and divides the data rate by that factor. Bit error rates use standard textbook formulas for each modulation over a plain noise channel. Raw rate is channel width times bits per symbol, divided by the spreading factor. Usable rate is raw rate times the chance that a 1,500-byte packet arrives with no bit errors. Real links add coding, interference, overhead, and many other effects that this lab leaves out.
