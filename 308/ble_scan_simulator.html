BLE Scan Simulator

Mobile and Wireless · BLE lab · practice environment

This is a practice environment, not a real scan.

Everything on this page is simulated. It does not use Bluetooth, your location, or your camera, and nothing you type leaves your browser. The numbers are built to behave like a real Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) scan so you can practice the same skills: finding advertising devices, reading signal strength, and reasoning about how often a device broadcasts. A real scan in a real room will look different and messier.

If your submission uses data from this simulator, say so in your document.

Step 1. Create your workspace

Type any word or phrase that identifies you, such as your Canvas name. The same text always builds the same simulated workspace, so your instructor can check your results. Different text builds a different workspace.

iPhones do not show Bluetooth addresses. They show a long identifier code that the phone makes up for each device. Choose the style that matches the device you would use in a real scan. You can change it at any time.

Step 2. Scan for BLE devices

Select Start scan and let it run until the scan clock reaches 30 seconds. In Fast mode that takes a few seconds. You can stop at 30 seconds or keep going (the scan stops itself at 2 minutes). Starting a new scan clears the list, like most scanner apps.

Phone scanners can save battery by listening less often. In the lower-power modes you will miss more packets, so counts drop and gaps between packets can look longer, just as on a real phone.

The list order changes when you stop the scan or change this setting, so rows do not jump around while you read them.

Step 5. Power explorer

This calculator estimates how long a battery lasts for a BLE advertiser compared with a Wi-Fi radio. Use the numbers in your written analysis. The starting values are classroom assumptions, not specifications for any product. You can change them. Check a real datasheet before you rely on them in a design.

Scenario Average current (mA) Runtime (hours) Runtime (days)

How the BLE number is made: average current = sleep current + (transmit current × radio-on time ÷ advertising interval). It is ideal math. Real batteries lose capacity to age and temperature, and a small coin cell cannot supply the peak current a Wi-Fi radio needs, so treat the Wi-Fi rows as a comparison of power demand, not as a design you could build.
